Direct jobs by role group

Jobs supported across the economy

Indirect jobs are in suppliers: hotels, transport, catering, set construction, equipment, printing, legal and security. Induced jobs come from workers spending their pay. Ratios are set at or below South Africa's measured film multipliers (NFVF 2017), because Uganda imports more of its inputs.

Tax and formality

A thin tax base, by structure

Most film enterprises sit below the UGX 150 million VAT threshold or on presumptive tax. The gap below is what full formalisation would yield, not a revenue forecast.

Estimated tax paid, UGX billion a year

Actual vs full-compliance potential

Rates: VAT 18%, withholding 6%, corporate income tax 30%, PAYE bands to 30% (PwC Uganda Tax Data Card 2025/26); film equipment import duty 10% plus levies (NPA 2025). Compliance shares are estimates. URA can replace this whole panel with aggregates by activity code.

Growth scenarios, central case

What policy could add by 2029

Baseline: no new policy, 9% nominal growth a year. NFIDP implemented: the proposed 2027 programme (28 films, 1,400 trained in seven regions), the proposed UGX 33.15bn National Film Fund and three regional hubs. Accelerated: NFIDP plus a foreign production incentive and single permit window. All programme figures are proposals, not approved funding.

Increment over baseline in 2029

The NFIDP's direct value-added effect is modest. Its stronger case is formal jobs, regional reach and the data it generates. The accelerated gain depends on foreign productions, which need an incentive and a film commission first.
